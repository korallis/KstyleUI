# Media provenance

Every file in `Media/` has a row here: where it came from, how it was made, and when. No file is copied from another addon.

## Fonts

All fonts are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; `Media/Fonts/OFL.txt` holds each project's licence text. The licence text sits beside the files. No font has a Reserved Font Name that the files below would break: Source Sans's "Source" applies only to modified versions, and its files are unmodified; Cinzel's licence names no Reserved Font Name, so renaming the modified Cinzel Caps and Cinzel Names is a courtesy, not a duty.

Lumen's faces come from [google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts) at commit `5bb28733cec313b63fbd415b574b5ae35c8aa8b8`, downloaded on 2026-11-09. `Tools/media/make_fonts.py` makes the five files from the sources with fontTools (4.68.0 for the first four; 4.60.2 gives the same bytes for Cinzel SemiBold, which was checked, and made Cinzel Caps and Cinzel Names), run from a throwaway virtual environment outside the repository (its docstring has the commands; `--only cinzel` needs just `Cinzel[wght].ttf`). It saves without touching the timestamp, so running it again gives the same bytes.

Why the digits are remapped: the game's text renderer applies no OpenType features, so a font always draws its default figures. Alegreya Sans's default figures are oldstyle and proportional (the 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 drop below the baseline, and each digit has its own width). The mockup asks for `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums`, and the font has `tnum` and `lnum` features the game cannot turn on, so the files point the digits straight at the tabular lining glyphs. Numbers then keep their width as they change, and no widget needs a fixed-width reservation for them. Cinzel's figures stay proportional; Cinzel is used for headings and captions, which do not count.

Why Cinzel is widened: the game has no letter-spacing call. `FontString:SetSpacing` is line spacing (Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/SimpleFontStringAPIDocumentation.lua; the UI source uses it only on wrapped text) and no method on a FontString or a Font, nor any attribute in `UI.xsd`, spaces letters. Spacing letters with thin spaces needs a thin-space glyph, which Cinzel lacks (the game draws a box). So the tracking is part of the face. Three faces, because the picture spaces its gold captions, its white headings and its large unit names differently. How they were measured (owner's picture, 3456 by 2234, every heading 15 px of cap height, which is the 10-unit role at 2.0685 px per unit): the glyphs were drawn from Cinzel at 20.6 to 20.75 px for tracking 0 to 0.12 em and weights 400 to 700, with subpixel offsets, and compared with the picture's ink by summed squared difference. At weight 500 the gold captions fit 0.07 em (SKYBORNE), 0.06 (QUESTS) and 0.11 (DAMAGE), 0.065 jointly, and the white headings 0.01 (WINDFIELD ORCHARD), 0.04 (LIGHTNING BOLT), 0.02 to 0.03 (SHADOWGALE POACHER) and 0.01 to 0.02 (POACHER LOOKOUT), 0.02 jointly. The fit's loss is flat from weight 400 to 600 (heavier weights are wider, so they want less tracking), so weight was chosen by the strokes: a stem of the capital I measures 1.35 px at weight 400, 1.94 at 500 and 2.52 at 600 at this size, and the picture's stems read 2.1 to 2.8 px including the browser's halo; side by side at 5 times, weight 500 is the closest to the picture's captions (400 is thinner, 600 bolder). Measured on the game-side render, SKYBORNE's ink spans 118 px at weight 400 and 0.07 em against the picture's 119. The 0.15 em that a first reading of the picture suggests would make the word wider than the picture's. The gold captions carry no drop shadow in the picture (the pixels beside each stroke on its lower right are as bright as those on its upper left), so the caps face is drawn without the kit's text shadow; the white names have a soft halo (the ring two pixels to a stroke's lower right is darker than the background, the upper left's is not), which the client cannot draw, so the names face keeps a lighter hard shadow (docs/core-visuals.md has the measurement and the source files). The large unit names were fitted the same way, with the size free: KORALLIS (the player's name) and SHADOWGALE POACHER (the target's) fit Cinzel at weight 500, 37 to 38 px, with -0.06 and -0.05 em of tracking (a negative advance pulls the letters together; with the font's kerning switched on the fits move to -0.04 and -0.02, and the game applies none), and the width of each name is within 4 px of the picture's at -0.055 em; the picture's browser kerns Cinzel's pairs and sets a wider word space, which the game cannot draw. `Widgets.ApplyFont` takes back the space the last letter leaves behind, a negative one for the unit face. The fonts have no thin space either, so nothing can space them with one. Build them with fontTools alone: with `uharfbuzz` installed fontTools packs GPOS differently and the bytes of the Cinzel files change, though the glyphs and advances do not; `make_fonts.py` refuses to run then, and `Tools/tests/test_fonts.py` checks that the files match the SHA-256 recorded here.

Lumen is the default font set: Cinzel SemiBold for display, heading and caption text, Cinzel Caps, Cinzel Names and Cinzel Unit (Medium, spaced) for the `caps`, `names` and `unit` roles, Alegreya Sans Tabular Regular for body text, Bold for numbers and Medium with an outline for text over the world. Source Sans 3 Semibold, Source Sans 3 and Inter stay selectable; picking one sets every role to that face, with Source Sans 3 Semibold (whose digits are tabular by default) for numbers.

## Procedural textures and logo

`Tools/media/make_media.py` draws these files. No image model or prompt is involved: each texture is a shape or a smooth function sampled 4x4 times per pixel and averaged. Run `python3 Tools/media/make_media.py` to redraw them and this table.

Shapes are white with their coverage in alpha, so the game tints them with `SetVertexColor`. Chamfers and bevels are drawn at 4 texels per root unit. A texture with slice margins is a nine-slice source: a corner piece of `m` texels draws at `m / 4` root units, so the corners of the fill and both bevel lines line up. Every row and column between the margins is the same, so the edges and centre stretch cleanly. The shadow is drawn at 2 texels per unit: its 48-texel corner pieces are 24 units, centred on the panel's corners. `python3 -m unittest discover -s Tools/tests` fails when a file differs from what the script draws.
