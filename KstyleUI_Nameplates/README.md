# KstyleUI_Nameplates

A KstyleUI plate on each nameplate: a clean health bar, name, the level diamond, cast bar, the player's debuffs, threat colour, target brackets and the focus edge, raid, elite and rare marks, in a friendly and an enemy style. Enemy plates can colour quest mobs, rares, elites and casters by type, cast bars can colour by whether the player can interrupt right now, the game stacks plates by the plate's own footprint, and one dropdown applies a preset.

Minimal keeps the shipped slim plate (120 by 9 under Medallions). Dungeon keeps each style's width (120 under Medallions, 156.75 under Frames and Orbs), with a 12-unit bar and an optional 11-unit health percent. Both keep 22-unit debuffs under Medallions (the existing larger 29-unit icons in Frames and Orbs); the Icon size slider still wins. Existing explicit Layout Mode sizes still win. The preset's stored `lumen` key remains compatible with saved profiles; its label is now Minimal.

Debuff timers show by default, centred under each icon in outlined number-role text (11 units under Medallions). Stacks keep their corner badge. The engine's duration binding turns time red below five seconds without reading aura durations in Lua. The Nameplates page offers the number-font switch, time and stacks switches, size sliders, and an expiring-colour threshold (zero disables it). Choosing the previous size, plate font and hidden time restores the old appearance.

Target and focus plates show an 11-unit spell name below the cast track. Only plates showing a name reserve its lane in their stacking footprint; target changes keep the health bars stationary and update downward debuff anchors. The selected-only toggle can show names on every plate. A ready interrupt or no known interrupt uses `gilt`; a known interrupt recharging or with unknown readiness uses `giltDim`. Uninterruptible casts use `steelDim` and a hollow shield glyph at the track's start even when spell icons are off. Secret interruptibility controls the glyph and colour through engine setters.

Target and focus swaps paint only their old and new plates and read the unit-frame cast settings once. Acquiring or clearing a target still refreshes other plates when their optional dimming must change. `test-nameplates-target-cost.lua` pins this cost; `test-nameplates-readability.lua` pins the cast cues and presets. `combat_readability_plates` renders ready, waiting and unknown readiness in three rows, with `RENDER_PLATE_PRESET=Dungeon` selecting the fuller preset.

## The bible's plate (2026-11-12)

The KSTY-44 design bible and its mock-up (`renders/KSTY-44/detail-nameplate-and-damage-numbers.jpg` in the Captain's drive) decide the plate now; Lee approved the mock-ups ("Ohh those look good") and asked for the whole overhaul at once. This replaces the 2026-11-09 picture's plate below wherever the two differ, and the rest of that section stands.

- **The bar** is the mock-up's 132 by 10 px at 1920x1080 and the shipped Interface size, **156.75 by 11.875** root units, with a one-physical-pixel black edge and one more pixel of 45 percent black outside it, and the kit's one-pixel top highlight. No ornament draws on or around it at any ornament level (`docs/ORNAMENT-SPEC.md`, Nameplates).
- **The target** shows **gilt brackets** at both ends, lit gilt (`giltLit`, the `target.color` default), 21.375 units tall with 5.9375-unit arms and a 2.375-unit stroke (at least one physical pixel), 7.125 units off the bar. They close in from 4.75 units out over 0.12 s, easing out, when a plate becomes the target, at full motion only, and never on a restyle. The target's bar keeps its black edge and its name stays white; the old gold outline and gold name are gone.
- **The level diamond** sits at the bar's left end, its right point one physical pixel clear of where the left bracket stands, so nothing moves when the target changes. It is a 21.375-unit square turned 45 degrees: a black outer pixel, a gilt rim, a black pixel and a dark face, with the level in lit gilt at 13 units (11 px at 1080p, the bible's floor). It shows by default.
- **Elite and rare marks** are on the diamond's rim, so rank never rests on colour alone: an elite, rare or world boss rim is two pixels instead of one, in gold (`neutral`), blue (`rare`) or red (`hostile`). With the level off, a marked unit keeps its diamond, empty.
- **The effect over the bar's end.** The player's effects grow left from the bar's right end, above the name, 29 units square with a black, red and black pixel rim; the stack count shows and the time left is off by default (a setting).
- **The name** is Cinzel caps at 16 units, 8 units above the bar, sized by its text, so a long name is never cut short.
- **The cast bar** runs under the plate, 2.375 units below the bar's edge, as before.
- Saved settings still at the 2026-11-09 defaults (name 12, level hidden at 16 with an outline, effects 26 growing right with timers at 12, health text 9, cast text 10, target colour `gilt`) and a saved plate size still at 151.56 by 13.78 follow the new defaults through Store migration 28; a value the player chose is kept.

## Rules

Personal resource display controls Blizzard's separate health and power display near the character (`nameplateShowSelf`). It defaults off; enabling the module applies the saved choice out of combat, and setup Apply turns it off only when the Nameplates addon is loaded. Keep my UI leaves it untouched. Changing this option writes the CVar too, including with the skin off. Presets and profile refreshes never reapply it. The player's own nameplate is not skinned, and a caster druid has no UnitFrames class-resource bars. `test-personal-resource-display` covers the boundary.

Combat cast cues can return to the previous single cast colour and no shield glyph. Spell name, selection-only names and names below the track are separate choices. UnitFrames > Player > Power bar > Text > Previous display restores hidden power figures in Frames/Medallions and the old centred percent in Orbs; None hides the readout. UnitFrames > Target > Lead with target percent restores current/max first when off. CombatText > Keep combat text clear restores the inherited player field and the ordinary target anchor when off.

These come from the [design spec](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-07-kstyleui-design.md). The roadmap's Global Constraints and [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) apply too.

- Attach a KstyleUI frame to each plate on `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_ADDED` and fade Blizzard's plate with alpha. Names of non-player units can be secret in combat, so they pass through whole.
- Cast bars use `StatusBar:SetTimerDuration` with the duration object from the cast APIs. Casts by units other than the player and pet can be secret, so the bar never reads the spell name except through widget setters.
- Auras use the AuraContainer engine (`CustomAuraContainerTemplate`, aura groups and filter strings). Never scan auras by index while `C_Secrets.ShouldAurasBeSecret()` returns true. Aura data turns secret during a boss pull before `InCombatLockdown()` returns true.
- At load the module only registers itself with `KstyleUI.Modules.Register`. It creates no frames, registers no events and installs no hooks until `Enable` runs. `test-modules-load.lua` checks the frames.
- Every element it shows registers with `KstyleUI.Layout.Register`, and every setting is declared through `KstyleUI.Options.Register`, so the player can resize and customise everything from the settings window.
- UI comes from `KstyleUI.Widgets`, colours from `KstyleUI.Tokens` and strings from `KstyleUI.L`. Translations live in the core string tables; see Languages in the repository README.
- Build mode: check that an API exists before calling it, check `issecretvalue` before any comparison, fall back to a safer path when the preferred one is unavailable, and log the path taken with `KstyleUI.DevTest.Log`.

## How a plate attaches

1. `Enable` registers the events and attaches a plate to every nameplate already shown (`nameplate1` to `nameplate40`).
2. `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_ADDED` gets the base frame with `C_NamePlate.GetNamePlateForUnit(unit)`. The player's own plate (the base `GetNamePlateForUnit("player")` returns) and plates that show only widgets (`UnitNameplateShowsWidgetsOnly`) stay Blizzard's. Forbidden plates, such as friendly plates in instances, never fire this event and stay Blizzard's too.
3. Each base frame gets one KstyleUI plate, made the first time and kept: a child of the base, anchored to its bottom. Base frames are reused, so the number of plates stays small.
4. Blizzard's `UnitFrame` on the base gets alpha 0, and so does its `selectionHighlight`, which ignores its parent's alpha. Its `SoftTargetFrame` gets `SetIgnoreParentAlpha(true)` instead, so the soft-target icon Blizzard draws there stays visible: the gamepad interact prompt (`SoftTargetIconInteract`, which gamepad mode turns on) and the enemy and friend icons the accessibility settings turn on. Blizzard still decides when the icon shows. A `hooksecurefunc` on the UnitFrame's `SetAlpha` puts 0 back when Blizzard's code sets the alpha. If the UnitFrame is not there yet, because KstyleUI's handler ran before Blizzard's driver, the fade waits one frame.
5. `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_REMOVED` hides the plate, gives the UnitFrame its alpha back and the `SoftTargetFrame` its own parent-alpha setting, because Blizzard's pool may hand it to a plate KstyleUI leaves alone.
6. `Disable` detaches every plate and restores every alpha it changed. The `SetAlpha` hook stays, as hooks do, and does nothing while the module is off.

Nothing on Blizzard's frames is reparented, moved, resized, hidden or scripted.

## What a plate shows

| Part | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health bar | `UnitHealthMax`, `UnitHealth` | Both go straight to `SetMinMaxValues` and `SetValue`; the engine eases the bar. A new unit on a reused plate snaps to its value. |
| Health text | `UnitHealthPercent(unit, true, CurveConstants.ScaleTo100)`, `AbbreviateNumbers(UnitHealth(unit))` | `SetFormattedText` takes the secrets. Without the curve the module builds its own, and without the curve API it shows the value. |
| Name | `UnitName` | Centred Cinzel caps at 12 units by default; the upper-case switch can preserve a readable name's original case. A secret name reaches `SetText` whole. Class-coloured names are opt-in, and take the class colour only when the bar does not already carry it (a reaction or custom bar colour, or a name-only plate); over a class-coloured bar the name reads white, so a pink name never sits on a pink fill. |
| Level diamond | `UnitEffectiveLevel`, else `UnitLevel` | On by default. A diamond left of the bar's left end, clear of the left bracket's place, drawn over the bar. The level reads in lit gilt; `-1` reads `??` in the hostile colour. |
| Elite and rare marks | `UnitClassification` | The level diamond's rim: two pixels instead of one, gold (`neutral`) for elites, red (`hostile`) for world bosses, blue (`rare`) for rares and rare elites. A secret classification marks nothing. |
| Raid marker | `GetRaidTargetIndex` | Always secret on Forever. `type(index)` tells nil from a number without reading it, and the index goes to `SetSpriteSheetCell` on Blizzard's raid icon sheet. |
| Threat | `UnitThreatSituation("player", unit)` | Readable on Forever. A secret status counts as none. See below. |
| Target and focus | `C_NamePlate.GetNamePlateForUnit("target")` and `("focus")` | Compared with the plate's base frame, which is never secret. When that call is refused or returns nothing while the unit exists, `UnitIsUnit` decides, and a secret answer counts as no. |

### Cast bar

The cast bar is a component: it adds itself to `ns.Components` with `Build`, `Restyle`, `Refresh`, `Clear`, `Room` (the height it reserves under the health bar) and the unit events it handles. It sits under the health bar as a flat six-unit line. Target and focus spell names are on by default, while icons and time left remain off. Names can return inside the track or be hidden. While the plate font follows the kit, the spell name reads in `body` and the time left in `time`, as the unit frames' cast times do; a chosen plate font applies to both. `cast.hideOnTarget` is off by default; switching it on suppresses the target plate's cast only while the UnitFrames target cast is enabled.

| Moment | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| `UNIT_SPELLCAST_START`, `UNIT_SPELLCAST_CHANNEL_START`, or a plate attaching to a unit that is casting | `UnitCastingInfo` or `UnitChannelInfo` gives the name, icon and `notInterruptible`; `type(name)` tells whether there is a cast without reading a secret. The bar runs from `UnitCastingDuration` or `UnitChannelDuration` through `SetTimerDuration`: casts fill (`ElapsedTime`), channels drain (`RemainingTime`). The name and icon reach `SetText` and `SetTexture` whole. There is no sheen or spark. |
| Time left | When enabled and the duration object itself is public, an `OnUpdate` on the bar writes `duration:GetRemainingDuration()` through `SetFormattedText` every 0.1 s. An opaque secret duration is passed only to `SetTimerDuration`, never indexed for a textual timer. |
| `UNIT_SPELLCAST_DELAYED`, `UNIT_SPELLCAST_CHANNEL_UPDATE` | The bar runs again from a fresh duration. |
| Interruptible or not | A plain flag picks the open colour or the uninterruptible colour, and dims the icon. A secret flag goes to `SetVertexColorFromBoolean` on the bar's texture and `SetAlphaFromBoolean` on the icon's shade, so the engine decides. `UNIT_SPELLCAST_INTERRUPTIBLE` and `UNIT_SPELLCAST_NOT_INTERRUPTIBLE` give a plain flag. The open colour is the cast colour (the accent or a custom colour) while the player's interrupt is ready, and the recharging colour (`cast.waitColor`, `giltDim` by default) while it is on cooldown: see Interrupt below. |
| `UNIT_SPELLCAST_STOP`, `_FAILED`, `_CHANNEL_STOP` | The bar fades out over `fade`. |
| `UNIT_SPELLCAST_INTERRUPTED` | The bar fills, turns `hostile`, says **Interrupted** and fades out over 0.8 s. The `STOP` that follows is ignored. |
| `UNIT_FLAGS`, `UNIT_FACTION` | These fire often in combat (combat state, stuns, roots, fear), so they rerun only the style, the bar colour and threat. A plate whose kind or name-only state changed refreshes in full, and even then a cast that is already running keeps going: the bar restarts only for a new cast ID, a settings change or a new unit. |

Without the duration APIs or `SetTimerDuration`, the bar takes the cast's start and end times as its range (they may be secret; the engine compares them) and an `OnUpdate` sets the value to `GetTime() * 1000`. That path shows no time text, channels fill rather than drain, and the devlog says it ran.

### Interrupt

Combat cast cues are on by default: ready or no known interrupt uses `gilt`, waiting or unknown cooldown readiness for a known interrupt uses `giltDim`, and uninterruptible casts use `steelDim` and a shield glyph. Switching the cues off restores the earlier single interruptible colour and no shield glyph. The ready, wait and shield colours remain settings.

On Forever every cast by a unit other than the player and their pet is secret (`SecretWhenUnitSpellCastRestricted`), so a plate can never match a cast's name or spell ID against a list of important spells. What a plate can know is what the player can do about the cast. The cast bar has three colours:

| State | Colour | How the plate knows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interruptible, the player's interrupt ready | `cast.color` (`gilt`, tan, by default) | `C_Spell.GetSpellCooldown(spell).isActive` is false, or `isOnGCD` is true while responding to `SPELL_UPDATE_COOLDOWN`; both fields are `NeverSecret` |
| Interruptible, the player's interrupt recharging | `cast.waitColor` | `isActive` is true and a trustworthy GCD-only result is unavailable; `SpellSharedDocumentation.lua` forbids trusting `isOnGCD` outside the cooldown event |
| Not interruptible | `cast.shieldColor` | the cast's `notInterruptible` flag, plain or through the engine |

`Interrupt.lua` finds the spell: the one named in `cast.interruptSpell` (a name or ID, resolved through `C_Spell.GetSpellIDForSpellIdentifier` when it can be, else handed to the cooldown API as written), or the first spell of `Logic.INTERRUPTS[class]` the player knows (`C_SpellBook.IsSpellKnown`, the player's and the pet's spell banks). The table holds Forever's own IDs, highest rank first: Pummel and Shield Bash, Kick, Counterspell, Earth Shock, Silence, Spell Lock, Rebuke, Skull Bash and Feral Charge. Hunters have no interrupt on Forever, so interruptible casts keep their normal gilt colour. `SPELL_UPDATE_COOLDOWN` rereads the cooldown once and, only when the state changes, computes the open colour once and recolours every running cast's fill with one widget call each (`Bar:SetFillColor`), skipping bars already in that colour; `SPELLS_CHANGED` finds the spell again. A secret or missing cooldown for a known spell stays unknown and uses the wait colour. Gaining or losing an interrupt spell repaints running casts even when readiness stays unknown. A readable active cooldown outside `SPELL_UPDATE_COOLDOWN` counts as recharging, so resolving during a GCD can briefly show "wait" until the next trustworthy event.

### Debuffs

The debuffs are a component too. Each plate gets one `CustomAuraContainerTemplate` container, made the first time the plate shows a unit with debuffs on, and kept. It has one aura group, `debuffs`:

| Part | How |
| --- | --- |
| Which auras | The filter string `HARMFUL\|PLAYER\|INCLUDE_NAME_PLATE_ONLY`: the player's harmful auras on the unit, including the ones the game marks for nameplates only. A setting drops `INCLUDE_NAME_PLATE_ONLY`. The engine finds, sorts and updates them; Lua never reads aura data, so `C_Secrets.ShouldAurasBeSecret()` never stops it. |
| How many | `maxFrameCount` is icons per row times rows. When the player has more debuffs on the unit than tiles, a chip one pitch after the first row says `+N`, up to `OVERFLOW_MAX` (16). The chip is the kit's overflow chip (`Widgets.Overflow`): a hidden counting container with the tiles' own unit and filter lays them out one per line, and the chip's label hangs from its foot, so the engine's own layout moves the count and Lua never reads it. It stays true in a boss pull or a rated match while aura data is secret. The chip is anchored to the health bar, not the container: a container with a group forbids layout on anything anchored to it. |
| Which win a tile | `debuffs.order` sets the engine's sort for both groups: `important` (`ImportantOnly`: the spells the game marks important, `C_Spell.IsSpellImportant`, first), `longest` (`ExpirationOnly` reversed), `expiring` (`Expiration`) or `default`. The engine sorts; Lua never reads a duration. |
| Layout | The engine's flow layout: horizontal lines `columns` icons long (`SetFlowLayoutMaximumLineSize`), growing right or left and up or down from the matching corner. Each row's line spacing holds its timer lane (`Auras.Lane`: a 1-unit gap, the text size and 3 units). The container hangs from that corner 4 units (`gapSmall`) plus the bottom row's timer lane above the name, or under the cast bar when rows grow down. |
| Unit | `SetUnit(unit)` when the plate attaches, `SetUnit("none")` when it detaches or debuffs are off. |
| Edit Mode | `SetEditModePreviewEnabled(false)`, so Blizzard's Edit Mode never fills plates with sample auras. |
| Buttons | `initializeFrame` builds each aura button before the engine restricts it: a square black border on an addon skin frame inside the button, the unmasked icon one physical pixel in, a square cooldown swipe, the stack count at the top right and the time left in its own lane under the tile, off the icon's art (round 5, Fable #3), each text on its own holder frame so a switch can hide it. `SetIcon`, `SetDurationCooldown`, `SetApplicationCount` and `SetDurationText` hand them to the engine. |

Aura buttons deny tainted access while auras are secret. A setting that changes the buttons (size, text, switches) restyles them only when `C_Secrets.ShouldAurasBeSecret()` is false, including a theme change. The static border has no tracked kit chrome that could read restricted dimensions. Otherwise the plate waits in one shared list, checked again after combat (`Combat.Queue`) or, when auras turn secret before combat starts, once a second, so a restyle never retries itself in a loop. The container's own layout changes at once. Without the engine, or when it refuses the container, plates show no debuffs and the devlog says why; a refusal in combat retries after combat.

Icon size and spacing snap to whole physical pixels (`Widgets.Snap` on the plate) before they go to the group layout, the flow line size and the buttons.

The settings window's preview draws three sample debuff tiles on the same snapped grid, because a preview has no unit for the engine. `Auras.Extent(plate)` gives how far a plate reaches above and below its health bar, debuffs included, and the preview spaces its sample plates by the tallest so no plate's debuffs run into the next. The pane clips its children, so when a large grid makes the stack taller than the pane, the preview shows only the plates that fit, at least one, and stacks again when the pane changes size.

The defaults are sized for a pull: 26-unit icons, five to a row (138 units, inside the 151.56-unit plate) and the `+N` chip one pitch after the fifth (164 units), with the time left and the stacks at 12 units, outlined, and the time left in a 16-unit lane under each row. More rows wrap more tiles, and the footprint the game stacks by grows with them (Stacking below), so neither the tiles nor the chip ever meet the name, the cast bar or the next plate. The owner judged the earlier 18-unit icons and 8-unit text too small to read in game on 2026-11-08. A plate shrunk for distance (`cvars.minScale` 0.8) still draws the icons at about 21 units. A size saved in a profile keeps its value.

### Unit types

An enemy NPC's bar, and the rim of its level badge, take a type colour over the reaction colour. One type wins, in this order:

| Type | Source | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quest | `C_QuestLog.UnitIsRelatedToActiveQuest(unit)`, else `UnitIsQuestBoss` | off, `quest` |
| Rare | `UnitClassification` is `rare` or `rareelite` | off, `rare` |
| Elite | `UnitClassification` is `elite` or `worldboss` | off, `neutral`: the gold diamond already marks elites |
| Caster | `UnitPowerType(unit)` is mana and `UnitPowerMax(unit)` is above 0 | off, `epic` |

Quest first because it says what to kill; rare before elite; caster last because the cast bar says the rest. Players keep their class colour, tapped mobs stay grey, and the health threat display still wins on the bar. Most melee NPCs carry the mana type with no mana, so the maximum decides; it is `SecretWhenUnitPowerMaxRestricted`, and a secret maximum changes nothing: the plate keeps the caster state it learned, and a plate that first meets its unit with a secret maximum shows no caster colour. `UNIT_DISPLAYPOWER` rereads the power type, and `QUEST_LOG_UPDATE` and `UNIT_QUEST_LOG_CHANGED` reread the quest state on every plate.

### Styles

An enemy NPC uses `plateHostile` for a readable hostile reaction; secret or missing reactions keep the kit's neutral fallback. A unit the player can attack (`UnitCanAttack`) uses the enemy style, any other the friendly style. Each style has its own bar colour source: the class colour for players and the reaction colour for NPCs, the reaction colour for everyone, or a custom colour. A player whose class is secret gets the secret class colour from `Theme.ClassColorForWidget`, passed straight to the bar and the name, and the plate's colour cache is skipped for it so the secret is never compared. A tapped enemy is grey (`textDisabled`). Friendly plates can show just the name. A secret answer keeps the bar.

## Stacking

The game stacks plates, and decides where a click lands, by one nameplate size (`C_NamePlate.SetNamePlateSize`, which Blizzard's `NamePlateDriverFrame:UpdateNamePlateSize` sets from the style CVars). With **Plate size for the game** on `KstyleUI's plate`, the default, `Stacking.lua` gives the game the plate's own footprint instead (`Stacking.Extent`, then `Logic.Footprint`): the bar, the level badge and the raid marker's room wide; the name, every row of debuffs with its timer lane, the bar and the cast bar tall, and never shorter than the level badge with its diamond; in the base's units (root units times `KstyleUI_Root`'s effective scale over `UIParent`'s, so the player's size setting resizes it through `ScaleChanged`). Stacked plates then clear each other, debuffs and cast bars included: Blizzard's own layout leaves a second row of debuffs out and lets it overlap, but the owner's rule is that no debuff ever runs into the next plate, so every configured row counts. The API is restricted in combat, so the size goes through `Combat.Queue`; a `hooksecurefunc` on the driver's `UpdateNamePlateSize` notes the size the game just chose and puts the footprint back; and `The game's own size`, or turning the module off, writes back the game's latest size. `gallery_nameplates_pull.lua` renders seven plates at that pitch, and `gallery_nameplates_clump.lua` eleven mobs on one point, stacked in one column at each plate's footprint times its scale (the stub has no engine stacking, so the scene places each plate where that rule puts it).

**Stacking bounds** (Forever 1.60.1 and later, KSTY-73). Where the client gives nameplates `SetStackingBoundsFrame` (`Blizzard_APIDocumentationGenerated/FrameAPINamePlateDocumentation.lua`, mirrored by `NamePlateScriptBaseMixin` in `Blizzard_NamePlates/Blizzard_NamePlates.lua`), `KstyleUI's plate` no longer resizes the game's nameplates. The game keeps Blizzard's own size, and each base gets a stacking-bounds frame of its own that covers the plate's whole footprint (the same `Stacking.Extent`, every debuff row included) down to the base's bottom, so stacked plates still clear each other. The bar sits where Blizzard's own health bar sits in that box: above the base's bottom by Blizzard's cast block and its spacing (`NamePlateSetupOptions` `castBarHeight`, `castIconHeight` unless the spell name is inside the cast bar, and `castBarToHealthBarSpacing`, as `Blizzard_NamePlateUnitFrame.lua:216-217` and `693-716` lay it out), or by the plate's own cast room when that is taller. The engine places, clamps and pushes the base, never the plate. Before this, the footprint box carried the name, a debuff row and its timer lane above a bar that sat low in it (10.5 units over the box's bottom at Lee's Large size, against Blizzard's 22.5, and 70 under its top against Blizzard's 54). So wherever the engine put the box (bottom on the head, centre on it, or its top held under `nameplateTopInset` in combat, which `nameplateShowOffscreen` keeps on screen), the bar landed 12 to 17 units below where Blizzard's would, and in combat it went below the mob's head while Blizzard's stayed above. `test-nameplates-head.lua` checks all three placements, in and out of combat, after Blizzard's driver sets its size, at every `nameplateSize`, screen and HUD size. A client without the call keeps the footprint as the nameplate size, as above. `The game's own size` points the bounds back at the base itself.

The engine multiplies that size by each base's own scale: `nameplateMinScale` to `nameplateMaxScale` with distance and `nameplateSelectedScale` on the target. With **Shrink distant plates** off, the default, the plate ignores that scale (Size and placement below), so at the game's defaults (the stub uses 0.8 and 1.2) the box the game stacked by grew and shrank while the drawn plate held its size: the column reflowed on every tab-target and kept re-solving as the player moved, which the owner saw in game on 2026-11-12 as plates darting around at 5120x1440. So while the module runs with Shrink distant plates off, `Stacking.lua` holds all three CVars at 1, as Blizzard's own commentator mode does (`Blizzard_Commentator/Mainline/Blizzard_Commentator.lua:231-234`), and the box matches the plate at every distance. It reads the character's CVars only after `VARIABLES_LOADED`, writes only through `Combat.Queue`, skips and logs a CVar the client lacks, and keeps the game's own value of each CVar it changed per character in the account data (`nameplateScaleCVars`). Turning Shrink distant plates on, or the module off, puts each value back unless the player changed it in the meantime; a login with the module off on that character gives them back through the core's CVar sweep (`Core/CVars.lua`). When Blizzard's driver resets the nameplate size in combat, which the restricted API cannot undo until combat ends, the devlog records it with the size the game chose.

## Size and placement

The plate's width and height belong to the Layout element `nameplates.plate`, a sample plate on `KstyleUI_Root`. It shows only in Layout Mode, where its handles resize every plate. Its **Width** and **Height** rows on the module's page in the settings window do the same. The element is not in the setup layouts: plates sit on Blizzard's plates, so the sample's position means nothing outside Layout Mode. **Height above the unit** moves every plate up or down.

Sizes are in KstyleUI root units. The game scales each base frame itself: C code sets the base's scale to `UIParent`'s effective scale times the plate's own factor (`nameplateGlobalScale`, `nameplateMinScale` to `nameplateMaxScale` by distance, `nameplateSelectedScale` on the target), and changes it per frame with no event. The plate is a child of the base, so by default it ignores its parent's scale (`SetIgnoreParentScale(true)`) and takes `KstyleUI_Root`'s effective scale as its own: a plate is exactly as large as the same units on the HUD at any distance, and nothing has to update as the base's scale changes. With **Shrink distant plates** on, the plate inherits the base's scale again and is scaled by `KstyleUI_Root`'s effective scale over the base's parent's effective scale times `UIParent`'s, so it draws in root units times the game's per-plate factor: the target's larger plate, distant plates shrinking. On a client without `SetIgnoreParentScale`, the default divides by the base's effective scale at each restyle instead, which the devlog says and which goes stale as the unit moves. The sample plate on `KstyleUI_Root` keeps scale 1. Blizzard's base frame is never scaled, sized or reparented.

## Why

- **Children of the base, not of the root.** A plate parented to Blizzard's base frame moves, scales, fades and hides with it, as the engine places and culls nameplates. A frame on `KstyleUI_Root` anchored to the base would show after the base hides and would miss the engine's occlusion and distance fades.
- **Fade, never hide or reparent.** Blizzard lays the UnitFrame out and owns it; hiding or reparenting it taints the nameplate driver. Alpha is not protected, and a post hook on `SetAlpha` is the one change to Blizzard's table, as the core's Skin helpers allow.
- **Base frames compared by identity.** `UnitIsUnit` can return a secret while unit comparison is restricted. A frame reference is never secret, so the target and focus come from `GetNamePlateForUnit` and an identity test.
- **One plate per base.** Base frames are pooled by the engine and reused for new units, so a plate is built once per base and refreshed on each attach. Nothing is built in a combat path after the first sighting of a base.
- **A sample plate for Layout.** Plates have no screen position of their own, but the spec wants every element resized in Layout Mode. The sample gives the player handles to drag; its size is the plates' size.
- **The engine shows the debuffs.** Aura data turns secret during a boss pull, before combat lockdown. The AuraContainer engine reads it in secure code and fills KstyleUI's own buttons, so the plates keep their debuffs through the pull without a Lua scan.
- **The engine runs the cast bar.** Enemy casts can be secret, so Lua never reads a cast's times or flags. Duration objects and the `FromBoolean` setters let the engine fill, time and colour the bar.
- **Threat only in a group.** Alone, every mob that attacks the player is on them, and a red glow on each says nothing new.
- **CVars back to the player.** Management, the scale hold and setup remember the player's own values across reloads. Turning them off restores only values KstyleUI still holds, so it leaves another addon's changes alone.
- **The footprint, not the overlap CVars.** `nameplateOverlapV` scales the game's own plate height, which stays Blizzard's; a plate twice that height would still overlap at any sane factor. Giving the game the plate's real size is how Plater and Platynator keep stacks apart, and the game's own driver exposes the same call.
- **Type colours on the bar, not a badge.** A pull is read at a glance by colour; a small quest or caster glyph would be lost at distance. One type wins so a bar never has to say two things.
- **No important-spell list.** Every nameplate cast is secret on Forever, so a list could never match. The interrupt's cooldown is the one fact the player can act on that the game lets a plate read in combat.

## Aura filters

The shared aura filter uses Only yours, Dispellable by you, Allow spell IDs, Block spell IDs and Sort by. IDs are positive whole numbers separated by commas or spaces; blank or invalid lists add no restriction. An allow list containing valid IDs limits the row to those spells within its other filters, and blocking wins. The engine handles identity filtering: an unreadable spell ID never becomes a Lua table key; an allow list rejects unidentifiable auras, while a block list alone cannot hide an unidentifiable spell. Overflow counters use the same filters as the visible icons.

Missing settings preserve only your debuffs, nameplate-only debuffs and the existing Important first order. Turn Only yours off to include other casters. The existing order key is retained. No saved settings are migrated.
