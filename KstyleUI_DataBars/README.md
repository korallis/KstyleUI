# KstyleUI_DataBars

XP, reputation and honour bars, micro menu, bag bar, and a data bar. The data bar starts docked beneath the action slab; picking Frames under HUD style puts it on a full-width top bar. The ordered Modules list places specialization and Hearthstone on the left, the clock in the centre and the other readouts on the right. The top bar defaults every module on except XP/reputation. The Medallions dock defaults to the original five readouts; the clock, frame rate and latency take the left instrument line (clear of the crest) and gold and durability the right, so both wing plates carry readouts.

Under **Data bar** in Options, choose Top, Docked under the action bars or Right rail, set height (12–32, default 14) and background opacity (0–100%, default 85%), choose Data bar artwork (Forged by default, with the existing gilt brackets and Medallions crest gem, or Plain glass), or turn the bar off. Top uses the screen edge in Frames and the slab's foot in Medallions. Picking Frames under HUD style chooses Top and shows the experience text as level, percent and rested at 12 units where those settings are still at their defaults (`HudStyle.FRAMES_LOOK`), and picking Medallions puts the defaults back. Store migration 45 moves a saved Top to Docked except on a profile explicitly saved on Frames; a player whose v0.2.12 or v0.2.13 update moved it picks Top again. The top and rail placements remain movable and resizable in Layout Mode; saved positions survive screen changes. The top bar spans the HUD root: the centred HUD band on ultrawide, or the whole screen under Spread. Its chrome uses the same card builder as the other cards and stays inside the bar rectangle without screen-edge bleed.

With **Menu and bag buttons in the card** on (the default), Frames docks the micro menu into the top bar's left zone as secure buttons up to 15 units, one unit inside the bar's top and bottom edges so no ring touches the screen's edge, with no tray; the bag bar keeps its own slot. The top menu stays visible. Turning that option off restores the standalone hover menu. In Medallions the menu returns to its own hover-faded slot, while the data bar sits on its own plate under the slab and uses the XP caption's font and baseline, including a custom font or the original prose face when Instrument experience text is off. The docked bar reads like XIV_Databar: each readout is a segment with a glyph from `Media/Glyphs.png` before its value (the sheet has no glyph yet for Coordinates, Combat, Time played, Audio, the professions or DataBroker, so they show their value alone), a gilt rule between visible segments but none beside a Spacer, Readout padding added to the room on both sides of every rule, a faint gilt highlight under the pointer and its usual tooltip and click. The XP caption is the last segment, with its own glyph; its rule shows only after a right-wing readout. Each slab wing has its own plate, parting under the crest hub; together they overhang the XP strip by four units at each end, sit at the strip's strata under the caption and the hub, and follow Data bar artwork (Forged: the steel card material; Plain: glass) and Background. If the client restricts the XP strip's layer, the plates keep their last safe layer; leaving the dock restores the bar's own strata and frame levels. The settings preview shows the same glyph segments on one plate. Data bar artwork shows for the top bar and the Medallions dock only: the right rail's optional Glass backdrop ignores it, and an explicit dock in Frames keeps the original dotted line with no plate. Left and centre share the left wing, and the right line ends one glyph, its gap and the four-unit segment pad before the XP caption. Reputation and honour strips sit fifteen units lower while docked, reserving the caption band. The dock follows the XP strip when the action bars resize. Docking and undocking wait for combat to end, including disabling the bar. The overlap guard exempts a readout from the hub only at its dock position; a readout moved onto the hub still fails.

At the default top position, top-anchored elements reserve `max(0, height + 6 - 16)` units, including custom saved top anchors. Moving the bar away or turning it off releases that space; returning it to the top restores it. Layout placement, rectangle queries and the overlap guard use the same inset. Store migration 41 turns the bar on in existing profiles while preserving a disabled DataBars module. It resets older rail geometry to the default placement and announces a moved readout once in chat. While a visible bar includes the clock module, the minimap foot hides its clock and its Clock toggle reads OFF and disabled; disabling that module, the bar or DataBars returns it.

## Rules

These come from the [design spec](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-07-kstyleui-design.md). The roadmap's Global Constraints and [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) apply too.

- Reskins hook with `hooksecurefunc` or `HookScript`, never `SetScript` on a Blizzard frame. They never write fields onto Blizzard frames, and never call Blizzard functions that open or refresh Blizzard windows. Per-frame state goes in `KstyleUI.Safe.Side(frame)`.
- Values Lua may read, such as XP, animate with `KstyleUI.Motion.Tween`.
- Nothing the module does to Blizzard's frames outlasts `Disable`: the status bars get their alpha and mouse back, and `Safe.RestoreBlizzard` returns the micro menu and the bag bar. Turning the module off needs no reload.
- At load the module only registers itself with `KstyleUI.Modules.Register`. It creates no frames, registers no events and installs no hooks until `Enable` runs. `test-modules-load.lua` checks the frames.
- Every element it shows registers with `KstyleUI.Layout.Register`, and every setting is declared through `KstyleUI.Options.Register`, so the player can resize and customise everything from the settings window.
- UI comes from `KstyleUI.Widgets`, colours from `KstyleUI.Tokens` and strings from `KstyleUI.L`. Translations live in the core string tables; see Languages in the repository README.
- Build mode: check that an API exists before calling it, check `issecretvalue` before any comparison, fall back to a safer path when the preferred one is unavailable, and log the path taken with `KstyleUI.DevTest.Log`.

Each element is a holder frame registered with Layout as resizable, at its footprint from `Setup.Layouts`. The holder never hides itself: a bar with nothing to show hides its `content` child, so Layout Mode still shows its plate. **The overhaul (KSTY-50).** Lee approved KSTY-44's mock-ups on 2026-11-12, and the design bible makes every bar clean (section 3.4: no end caps, no rails, nothing around a bar) with experience as a 5-unit strip. So the experience bar is now 491 by 8.3125 (the mock-up's 7 px at 1080, KSTY-60 item 28; it was 5) with square ends (the bar widget's pill masks off on every data bar, the reputation and honour bars too), the mock-up's purple (`custom`, the `epic` token; the gilt filament stays a choice), ticked into ten segments, with the `hudRested` rested shade at 55% and its caption on the world (the `WORLD` outline: a one-pixel outline with the kit's shadow). No data bar draws a forged ornament piece any more: the experience, reputation and honour bars no longer own an ornament handle or the rim frame it read. Store migration 24 moves a profile that kept the old defaults (the gilt source, no segments, no outline, an opaque rested colour, a saved 8-unit strip) to the new ones and leaves any other choice. The strip keeps its foot at 36.5, so the caption does not move and the gap to bar 1 grows from 9.5 to 12.5.

LEE-20 (2026-11-09, "let the picture win") replaced the old four-unit segmented XP line; until the overhaul the default was 491 by 8 with rounded ends, no border or ticks, and an opaque `hudRested` blue rested reach. Its body-font caption is centred below: "Level 11 · 0% · rested 5%" (`levelRested`). LEE-71 raises the old six-unit caption gap by 1.15 snapped root units and defaults XP to Flat, so the rested blue reaches the picture's token colour instead of being dimmed by the texture. Store migration 15 rewrites a saved XP Glass key or path to Flat once. The picker and both XP layers use that same value; a later Glass choice draws Glass and survives reload. Other selected textures and reputation/honour paint remain unchanged. This supersedes #204's omission of the level. Colour, border, segment and caption settings remain available; `percentRested` still omits the level. Earned progress retains the selectable gilt gradient. A label placed on a horizontal bar has a small glass plate; a label below it needs none.

## Data bar readouts

The ordered **Modules** list controls each module's visibility, Left/Centre/Right zone and order. The top bar defaults all modules on except Experience and reputation. The Medallions dock keeps its own ordered list, with frame rate, latency, gold, durability and time on by default; additional readouts remain available in the same settings. The left zone starts with specialization and Hearthstone; the centre holds the clock; the right holds guild, friends, durability, bag space, frame rate, latency, gold and tracked currencies. The micro menu keeps its own holder until the docking batch. Its ordered-list switch and child settings already control that holder.

`Readouts.lua` extends the strip's existing value, tooltip and action registries. Every readout uses the strip's shared font, unit spacing, middle dots, hover and tooltip builders. Guild hides without a guild, specialization hides without two groups, and currencies hide when nothing is tracked. The clock yields back to the minimap when its module or the bar is hidden; the minimap clock row follows the same ownership check.

Secret or missing readings display `--`; secret values are checked before comparisons, arithmetic or formatting. Protected proxies attach only outside combat, and the tooltip explains that the action is available after combat. In game, confirm that out-of-combat SetActiveSpecGroup switches both groups without ADDON_ACTION_BLOCKED/FORBIDDEN or taint; the talents proxy is available when the switching API is missing, and cannot automatically detect a protected-call refusal. Each item is built only when the bar first shows that module; hidden bars build no readouts. Readout events register only while that module is on and the bar is visible. Account-gold work waits for a money event or tooltip; roster requests wait for the guild tooltip. Value-only events update values without repainting the bar, and the one-second ticker covers the Hearthstone cooldown. Units follow their values in lowercase, currencies use middle dots, and the Hearthstone uses the shared short duration format or `ready`. The options window writes `strip.modules` for the top bar, `strip.railModules` for Right rail and `strip.dockModules` for the Medallions dock. Right rail defaults to frame rate, latency and gold in the v0.2.6 slot order. The rail and each dock wing keep a prefix of its module-list order that fits, dropping whole items from the end without shrinking or truncating text. The last visible item lists the hidden readouts in its tooltip.

`test-databars-readouts.lua` covers values, tooltips, secrets, unavailable APIs, secure proxy attributes, roster throttling, persisted character gold, the actual ordered-list model and the minimap hand-off at all four owner screens. `hud_reference` renders the full bar in Frames and the dock in Medallions, with `RENDER_DATABAR_ARTWORK=forged|plain`.

## Legacy data strip

`databars.strip` is the readout, registered with Layout at 278 x 12 (`Strip.LINE` and `Strip.COMPACT`), at the right rail's foot, 6 units over the damage card. By default it is one line of plain text with no card, right-aligned on the element's right edge and centred in its 12 units, as the owner's picture draws it: "162 fps · 15 ms · 26s 16c". Each item is its value followed by its unit in the same face, size and `text` colour (the picture's #c3cbd4 measures as this grey-white, not `textEmphasis`; LEE-70), and the items are joined by a middle dot (U+00B7, written as the decimal escape `\194\183` in the source; every bundled face has the glyph). The line reads at 12 units in the body face, one unit above the font's own centring so the baseline lands on the picture's, with the same blank on both sides of every dot: a third of the font's em, snapped to whole pixels by `Widgets.DotGap` (5/21 on a damage row) and never under one pixel, as the KSTY-44 mock-up spaces "120 fps · 17 ms". The old 4/21 snap with a pixel taken off each side left `fps` touching its dot at 1080p (KSTY-60 item 28). The picture draws oldstyle proportional figures and the kit's body face has tabular lining ones, so the values sit a few pixels left of the picture's ink; the labels are the measure. Each item is its own button covering just its text, so it keeps its KstyleUI tooltip and its click: the secure proxy covers the hovered item's text, not the line. A value that grows on a tick pushes the items left of it further left, and the right edge stays put.

**The picture wins over #211 (LEE-20, 2026-11-09).** The readout was a 318 x 42 glass card of five fixed cells (the frame rate in the accent, durability as "96% armour", the clock off), docked at the rail's foot under the damage card with the menu rows hung under its line. The owner's picture draws a plain line above the damage card with no durability, so the defaults changed and the old looks stayed as settings: `strip.line` (on), `strip.backdrop` (off), `strip.valueColor` (`plain`), `strip.menus` (now on by default) and `strip.fontSize` (11). With `strip.line` off, or the strip turned into a column, the strip is the cells layout described next, on glass again if `strip.backdrop` is on; turning the line off grows the element to 42 units tall so a caption and a value fit, turning it on returns it to 12. The checks in `test-databars-strip.lua` pin the line, then draw the cells on the composition mockup's 492 x 43 strip.

The cells layout holds up to five items, each in its own fixed cell: a caption in the text grey, 2 points smaller than the value (never below the `caption` size), over a value in white (or graded), on a button that lights under the pointer and shows a KstyleUI tooltip, so the values lead when the player scans it.

In the cells layout the cells share the width inside one content inset by weight (`CELL_WEIGHT` in `Strip.lua`): frame rate 0.8, latency 0.9, gold 1.4, durability 1.25, time 0.85. A cell depends only on the strip's size and the items shown, never on a value, so a frame rate that falls from 144 to 9, or gold that grows, moves nothing. A value wider than its cell is cut short, and its tooltip carries the whole value. The readable minimum is 4 ems of the value size per unit of weight, about 250 units for five items at 12; the settings preview scales its sample strip down below that. The captions step aside, and the values sit on the centre line, when the caption and value together are taller than the strip inside its edge lines (a large font) or when any caption is wider than its cell (a narrow strip). No separators by default; turned on, a separator is a hairline at a cell's left edge, 40% of the strip's height, in the text grey at 35%. In a column each row has its caption at the left and its value at the right.

The tooltips add detail: home and world latency and bandwidth, money earned or spent this session, each equipped slot's durability, and local and realm time. One `C_Timer.NewTicker` of 1 second runs while a timed item shows and stops with the module; a client without it counts seconds with an `OnUpdate` on the strip. A tick reads only the timed items (frame rate, latency and the clock) into preallocated tables and moves items only when a text's width changed. Gold and durability update on their own events. The clock reads `date("%H")` and `date("%M")`, so a tick builds no date table.

An XP, reputation or honour event repaints only a bar's values, colour and text. Textures, orientation, ticks and the font are restyled only when a setting, the layout, the profile, the theme or the scale changes.

## Micro menu and bag bar

`databars.micro` and `databars.bags` are KstyleUI's own bars, registered with Layout as scalable holders at their footprints from `Setup.Layouts`: the micro menu above the data strip and the bag bar above it, bottom right. While a bar is on, `Safe.HideBlizzard` hides Blizzard's: `MicroMenuContainer`, the Edit Mode frame that holds `MicroMenu`, and `BagsBar`.

Both bars are on by default and out of sight at rest, because the owner's picture shows neither (the Captain's ruling on #221, under "let the picture win its what i want", LEE-20; #197 had hidden them outright). At rest each bar's content frame has alpha 0; it fades in (`Tokens.Duration("fade")`) while the pointer is over one of its buttons or anywhere on its row, and back out 0.2 s after the pointer leaves the row (a hover hook on each button and on the row, which takes mouse motion but not clicks through `SetMouseMotionEnabled`, set out of combat because it is a protected call; then a `C_Timer.NewTicker` that reads `IsMouseOver` until the pointer is gone). A profile switch recomputes the fade. Both stay in sight while Blizzard's Edit Mode is on (`EventRegistry` `EditMode.Enter` and `EditMode.Exit`, `EditModeManagerFrame:IsEditModeActive`) or KstyleUI's Layout Mode is open; the bag bar while a bag is open (`IsAnyBagOpen`, `BAG_OPEN`, `BAG_CLOSED`, the bag toggles, or KstyleUI's bag window shown); the micro menu while the game points at one of its buttons (a micro button that `IsPulsing` or `HasNotification`, or a help tip in the `MicroButtons` system, `HelpTip:IsShowingAnyInSystem`). When a button pulses and nothing else lights the menu, only that button shows, at its place in the row, with the others and the tray at alpha 0 until the pointer comes; when the pulse ends with the pointer away, the lone button fades out with the row and the others come back only when the row next shows (KSTY-34's audit: a levelling character's talent point kept the whole row lit); a help tip with no pulsing button still shows the whole row, because the game does not say which button it points at. `menus.always` keeps both in sight. The fade only sets the alpha of KstyleUI's own content frame: `SetAlpha` carries no `IsProtectedFunction` flag in `SimpleFrameAPIDocumentation.lua` (70862), where `SetPoint` does (`SimpleScriptRegionResizingAPIDocumentation.lua`), so it runs in combat on a frame that holds secure buttons; at alpha 0 the buttons still take clicks and keybinds, as the chat card's buttons do. Nothing hides, reparents or fades a Blizzard frame. Switched off (`micro.shown`, `bags.shown`), a bar shows no buttons, is not live in Layout Mode, and Blizzard's bar stays hidden through `Safe`, so nothing returns to the bottom centre. The game's keybinds open every window as before (B, C, P, N, L, J, Escape and the rest call Blizzard's toggles, not the micro buttons), and the readout's gold and durability still open the bags and the character sheet through the secure proxy. Neither setting hides, moves or touches a Blizzard frame. Docked, the secure buttons make the strip's frame protected, so a row turned on or off in combat waits, with the element's height, for combat to end (`Combat.Queue`), as the buttons' own placing always has.

Each KstyleUI button is a `Widgets.IconButton` made from `SecureActionButtonTemplate`, with `type = "click"` and `clickbutton` set once, when the bar is built, to Blizzard's own button. A click runs Blizzard's handler from secure code, as a click on Blizzard's button would, so the buttons work in combat.

## Why

- **Our own bars, Blizzard's faded.** Blizzard's experience and reputation containers sit in Edit Mode's bottom bar stack, which Blizzard lays out together with the protected action bars. NaowhForever hid those containers and tainted the action bars. DataBars therefore never hides, reparents, moves or hooks them: it sets `StatusTrackingBarManager`'s alpha to 0 and turns off the mouse on each bar, so the invisible bars cannot show a tooltip or catch a click over KstyleUI's HUD. `Disable` restores both.
- **Readable values only.** Experience, reputation, rank points and honour are not secret on Forever. The bars still check `issecretvalue` before any comparison, and a secret value hides its bar and logs once, so a future client change cannot raise an error.
- **Honour is Forever's PvP rank.** Forever loads Blizzard's honour bar, but its PvP progression is the rank points of major faction 2800, which Blizzard's own PvP rank panel reads. The honour bar shows those points and the rank, and falls back to `UnitHonor` when the faction returns nothing. The bar has its own element key, `databars.honor`, under the reputation bar; the experience and reputation bars moved up 3 and 4 units to make room.
- **A holder and its content.** Layout owns the holder's visibility for the player's shown rule. The module shows and hides only the content, so the two never fight over one frame.
- **One event and one callback hub.** `KstyleUI.Events` and `KstyleUI.Callbacks` keep one handler per owner and event. Several bars listen for `UPDATE_FACTION` and `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`, so `Init.lua` collects every handler and registers one dispatcher per event when the module starts. `Modules` removes them all when it stops.
- **The micro menu and bag bar are replaced, not moved.** Forever's Edit Mode puts Blizzard's micro menu at the bottom centre (`MICRO_MENU_ANCHOR_OFFSET_X = 116.5` in `Camelot/EditModePresetLayoutConstants.lua`) with `BagsBar` beside it, on top of KstyleUI's action bars and data bars, and the spec places frames with KstyleUI's Layout Mode. Layout never moves a Blizzard frame, so, as the spec says for a Blizzard frame that needs a new place, `Safe` hides Blizzard's and KstyleUI's own bars take their place. `MainActionBar` and `BagsBar` anchor to `MicroMenuContainer`; reparenting keeps every anchor, so nothing protected moves, and `Safe` waits for the end of combat and of Edit Mode. DataBars hides the container, not `MicroMenu`, because Blizzard puts `MicroMenu` back into its container after vehicles and pet battles (`ResetMicroMenuPosition`). It never moves, hides or hooks `OnShow` inside either tree, and it writes no field onto a Blizzard frame.
- **Each button clicks Blizzard's own.** Opening a window from addon code would call Blizzard functions that open Blizzard windows, which the reskin rules forbid and which can taint the panel code. A `SecureActionButtonTemplate` with `type = "click"` instead runs Blizzard's button's handler from secure code. Unlike the strip's single proxy, which moves to the hovered item and so cannot follow the pointer in combat, every micro and bag button is its own secure button, so bags open in combat.
- **Strip clicks reach Blizzard's buttons through a secure proxy.** Opening bags, the character sheet or the calendar from addon code calls Blizzard functions that open Blizzard windows, which the reskin rules forbid and which can taint the bag and panel code. Instead one `SecureActionButtonTemplate` button, parented to `UIParent` so no KstyleUI frame turns protected, covers the hovered strip item out of combat with `type1 = "click"` and `clickbutton1` set to Blizzard's own button. The click then runs Blizzard's handler untainted. `PLAYER_REGEN_DISABLED`, which fires before lockdown, puts the proxy away; in combat the items keep their tooltips and say the action waits for combat to end. A right click passes through the proxy's `PostClick` hook to the item's own action.
- **Fonts outside the kit's restyle list.** The kit restyles every text made with `Widgets.Text` to its role's font on `ThemeChanged`, which would undo a player's chosen font. Bar texts are plain font strings that `Font.Apply` styles, and the module applies them again on `ThemeChanged`, `ScaleChanged` and `ProfileChanged`.
